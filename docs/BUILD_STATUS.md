| 2 Shared foundations | **On main** | Money keeps exact cents (a JavaScript number is rejected). Dates use the employer's time zone. A change or a correction keeps one history row per day, and a later edit does not silently overwrite a future row. Each request sets the tenant, the user, and the roles on its database transaction. | 3 Platform services | **On main** (pull request 2) | Sign-in checks the Supabase token, then loads the person from the directory (a token cannot name its own organization). The identity app creates, updates and disables users, and a client approves an outside payroll provider. A manager does not see home addresses or SINs. Audit rows hash the same way as the reference file. An hourly job seals those rows, signs the batch, and stores the signature where it cannot be overwritten. A former employee who left on 15 November 2026 keeps their own documents until 28 February 2028. |
| 4 Change sets, events, and live updates | **On main** (pull request 3) | Every edit is a change set with an effective date. Checking it lists errors, warnings, and what else will change, and it writes nothing. Saving writes the history, the audit, and the event together, or it writes nothing. A past date asks whether this is a new fact or a correction. A future raise stays in place when a transfer changes a different field, and the new team is carried onto that raise. A different future rate asks you to keep, replace, or merge it. A transfer whose budget impact is over $10,000 goes to HR and then to finance. You cannot approve your own request, and someone who also reports to you on another job cannot approve you. Each day the latest audit seal can be copied to the customer. An event is delivered once, even if the sender restarts, and after ten failures it can be replayed. Open screens hear only the identifiers of units they are allowed to see. |
| 5 Core HR | **On main** (pull request 4) | Hiring, a second job, rehire, and leaving are checked in memory. A Manitoba work location sets the tax province and a TD1 task. A rate under a caller-supplied floor is refused and the rule source is named. SIN is encrypted, shown as a mask, and revealed only with a reason. A position over the remaining budget goes to finance. A second full-time holder is blocked unless an overlap is approved. Leaving opens the position on that date and asks the manager to backfill, change, or remove it. Moving a unit is one operation, including a 3,000-person unit, and assignment rows stay as they were. Switching on positions keeps the same reporting lines. An Inside job picks up the Inside agreement, and an exclusion needs a reason. Seniority sorts by seniority date, then hire date, then lot, and the list records the challenge end. A home-address province change asks whether work moved and does not change the tax province on its own. A legal name waits for a document and HR. An acting job suspends the home job until the end date. Level rules and the org subtree honour the date. People, the person profile (six tabs), and positions are on the site in English and French. The rows you see are samples. |
| 6 Org chart | **On main** (pull request 5) | The chart is a view of the same records as the profile. Choosing Move to… writes a change set (the same call a drag would make). A move dated next Monday leaves today on the old manager and Monday on the new one, and timesheets follow that date. Another open chart hears that the unit changed. A past move into another province shows the tax change and a retro request for each person, after approval. Two people cannot fill one full-time vacant box unless an overlap with an end date is approved. A plan of five moves leaves the live chart alone until the plan is approved as one change set. A December transfer keeps a January raise and carries the new team onto it. A section move dated 30 days out previews approval chains, cost centres, security scopes, and seniority groupings, and nothing changes before that date. Five thousand boxes lay out in under two seconds. The chart on the site is a list of sample boxes, in English and French. A drag canvas and the browser test suite are not in this slice. The seven chart behaviours and the section-move preview are checked by the package tests. |
| 7 Rules engine | **On main** (pull request 6) | Agreements say how overtime, holidays, and premiums combine. Every sample case matches the reference, including the totals. A version that does not say what happens when weekly overtime falls on a holiday cannot be turned on. A premium in the regular rate cannot stay flat on overtime. Ontario, British Columbia, and Alberta read their minimum wage and overtime from the employment-standards file. An agreement that pays overtime sooner, or more vacation, wins, and the answer names the article or the statute. Part-time seniority points follow the agreement's own points, hours, and yearly cap. A minimum-wage change proposes a new grid rate on the same date. The employment-standards file is still marked "check before use", so it cannot be turned on for real. The extra agreement fields are on main (pull request 7). The agreement tables exist. The rule files are not loaded into them yet. |
| 8 Payroll calculation | **On main** (pull request 8) | One pay period for one person, outside Quebec, matches the reference to the cent. The 77 sample pays pass, including the published bonus example. Random pays in every other province and territory match the Python reference. The same pay produces the same result fingerprint every time. Amounts come from the statutory files for the pay date. Nothing is posted to a pay run. |
| 9 Pay runs | **On main** (pull request 10) | A pay date on a weekend or a bank holiday moves to the previous banking day, and a move into the prior year is flagged. Approval needs a complete route and a different person. One failed check holds that person; the organization can instead stop the whole run. The first live run waits for an accepted parallel run. Vacation pay follows the position. A pay group waits until proration is chosen. Only verified starter codes are copied. Support is taken before the organization's deductions, and pay stays at or above the protected minimum. Year-to-date CPP and EI stay on the person and the payroll account. A second open run for the same person and account is refused and names the run that already has them. A simulation takes no lease and writes no year-to-date. Calculation is split into buckets of about 500 people. A worker claims a bucket with a row lock, and a repeated message for a finished bucket writes nothing. Year-to-date is written only when the run is finalized. The same person produces the same result fingerprint whether the run is one bucket or many. The site still does not run a pay. The database connection exists, and the site Worker attaches it, but pay-run code does not open a live run yet. |
| 10 Retro | **On main** (pull request 13) | A committed past change to rates or related pay inputs opens one retro request for each finalized period that differs. The difference is paid later as a non-periodic line, taxed in the year it is paid, and only after the retro run is approved. Eight thousand people are split into batches of 500, with a progress view of how many are done and what it costs. A former employee is included when the agreement says so, and an application after the window is left out. A payment of $3,000 or more that reaches a prior year produces a T1198 letter when the rules qualify. Reversing a finalized off-cycle payment negates the result, the year-to-date, and the general ledger. A closed grant is charged to its successor, its fallback, or suspense, and the amount is listed for the funder. A period before go-live reads what was paid from the migrated history. The nightly check recalculates a sample from the stored fingerprint. A mismatch is a severity-1 alert and blocks that engine version. It does not change the stored pay. A repeated batch message writes nothing the second time. |
| 11 Time and leave | **On main** (pull request 14) | Overtime follows the province and the agreement, and the person is paid whichever gives more. A 13-hour day in British Columbia is 8 regular hours, 4 at time and a half, and 1 at double time. Alberta keeps the larger of the daily and weekly overtime totals. Ontario weekly overtime is added across jobs with the same employer and paid on the job where the 44-hour line was crossed, at that job's rate. An agreement that starts overtime sooner is used when it pays more. New Brunswick overtime uses minimum wage when that is the rule. A sample night shift pays $324.25 and a sample holiday shift pays $447.33. Punches stay on the exact minute unless the organization turns rounding on, and a check of 10,000 punch sets shows rounding does not systematically shorten the day. A meal break comes off only when it was punched or confirmed. Extra hours are paid even when nobody approved them. A timesheet still open at the cut-off is locked and paid as recorded. A correction after that is a later adjustment dated to the original day, or an off-cycle pay when the law's deadline would be missed. Rest breaks and late schedule changes need a written reason. Leave balances, each plan's own year, protected reasons, the attendance program, and the team availability bar follow the owner's rules. Banked time can be carried, paid, or both, and a payout goes through the organization's route. The site does not capture time yet. |
| 12 Disbursement, remittances, and the ledger | **On main** (pull request 15) | An approved pay becomes a bank file only when two different people release it. The file matches a saved sample for RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, CIBC, National Bank, Desjardins, and a credit union. Those samples are CanDo's own test files for a 1,464-character deposit layout and one transfer message. They are not files the banks sent, and the data-centre numbers in them are samples. A checksum is kept with the file. The file is stored in pieces and put back together to the same bytes, in memory. Sending the file by SFTP is not built. Remittance due dates follow the remitter type. Reminders go out 7, 3, and 1 days before the due date, and they stop once the payment is recorded. Work in Nunavut or the Northwest Territories withholds the payroll-tax rate supplied for that pay, and that amount stays off the T4. A current employee gets the pay statement and the tax slip in self-service. Paper is used when they ask, and for a former employee or someone on leave or laid off who has not agreed to electronic delivery. Email is used only with consent. A pay line with no cost coding uses the assignment, then the position, then the unit. If none of those has coding, it goes to the suspense account and finance gets a task. Pay is not held for that. A grant that has ended, or that hits its cap, sends the rest to the fallback fund. Finance is reminded 90, 60, and 30 days before the grant ends. A pay that crosses 31 March splits the ledger and the budget across the two fiscal years. Each ledger line keeps the work period, the posting period, and the mapping version in force on the work date. A closed period posts into the next open period and still names the work period. At period end, unpaid days are accrued and reversed on the first day of the next period, and a change in a liability is posted. A person paid by one organization and working for another produces a recovery for the cost, the cost plus a markup, or a fixed fee. Journal files are prepared for QuickBooks, Sage, Xero, Dynamics, NetSuite, and a plain spreadsheet. The site does not build a bank file or post a journal. |
| 13 Year-end, records of employment, and go-live import | **On main** (pull request 16) | A T4 and a T4A are checked against the schema files CRA published. Those files are stored in the repo with the page they came from and the day they were downloaded. CanDo did not write the schemas. A submission holds one kind of slip. Empty optional tags are left out. An amended slip uses the amendment code. Before filing, anyone whose CPP or EI does not match the calculated amount is listed. A record of employment is a draft with insurable hours, earnings by pay period, the reason code from the caller's list, and a due date. The file is built only after HR reviews it, and it is checked against Service Canada's payroll-extract schema. The schema file is Service Canada's, downloaded with its source page. If one job ends and another job on the same payroll account still has earnings inside the caller's window, no record of employment is issued, the leave plan for the ended job is flagged to settle, benefits are flagged to be looked at again, and the position is opened. If the last job on the library account ends, that account gets a record of employment and the city job is left as it is. Someone who leaves on 15 November 2026 keeps their own slips until 28 February 2028, or until 28 February 2029 when the organization chose 24 months. A move to CanDo on 1 July produces one T4 for the year. The imported amounts and the CanDo amounts are added, and both count toward the CPP and EI maximums the caller supplies. Days the old system already paid are left out of the first CanDo period. Opening year-to-date, pay-period history, balances, liabilities, and funding spent are loaded only when each line matches the old register to the cent, through one import change set. The site does not file a slip or upload a record of employment. |
